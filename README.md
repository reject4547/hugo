# Hugo

Hugo is a browser extension that blocks the domains of companies that have supported the Trump administration.

These companies have collectively donated hundreds of millions of dollars to Trump's campaigns and political activities. It's time to hold them accountable for their actions. Authoritarianism thrives on the support of businesses and billionares, and they won't change until it impacts their bottom line.

The simplest form of protest you can make is to simply not engage with these companies. Don't buy their products, don't use their services, don't give them clicks and page views.

## FAQ

*** Q: Does this really do anything? ***

A: Maybe, maybe not. But it costs you nothing to avoid these companies, and demonstrates that there are consequences for supporting authoritarianism. You absolutely can and should take part in other forms of resistance and activism, but where your money and your attention goes matters.

*** Q: What sites does Hugo block? ***

A: Hugo blocks the domains of companies that have directly supported the Trump administration, or that are owned or operated by oligarchs who have supported the Trump administration. You can find the complete list in the `config.json` config file.

*** Q: Can I submit additional domains to the blocklist ***

A: Absolutely! Unfortuantely for us all, the scale of corruption is so vast that it's hard to form an exhaustive list of Trump's backers. Post a PR adding new domains to `config.json`. You _must_ include sources for their affiliation to the Trump administration.

*** Q: Can I allow a blocked site? ***

A: Yes, Hugo is designed to encourage you to make intentional choices about your browsing habits, and [nudge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nudge_theory) you toward better choices. But reality is complicated, and it's better to make limited compromises than to give up entirely. 

Nothing will stop you from disabling the extension entirely if you need to, so Hugo has been designed to be flexible. You can bypass Hugo's checks for a given domain temporarily with a single button click, or add a permanent exemption. You can also temporarily disable all blocking for a set period of time.

*** Q: Why is it called Hugo? ***

A: Obviously, it's in honor of brilliant comedian [Hugo Boss](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/entertainment-arts-51703859) and definitely not any other Hugo Boss who we may draw historical parallels to.

*** Q: Does Hugo track my traffic? ***

A: No, the extension collects no information about you. Your web browser and your ISP may not be so kind.

*** Q: Can a domain be removed from the blocklist? ***

A: Absolutely. If a company posts a public renouncement of the Trump administration, and makes a sizeable donation to the ACLU (at least $10M or 10 times their donations to Trump, whichever is greater), and fires any executive who has supported Trump, their domain will be removed from this list. A PR with proof is required for removal.

*** Q: Why isn't Google on this list? ***

A: While Google has also contributed to the Trump inaugural fund, blocking the Google domain means making it harder for users to disable the extension in the Chrome Web Store. This is an unfortunate reality of the modern browser landscape, but should not be seen as an endorsement of Google.
